# Validation: do simulated respondents answer like real people?

This page reports how closely Undercurrent's simulated populations reproduce real Americans' answers to real survey questions. Every number here comes from the run recorded with it; the tables between the markers are generated by `scripts/validate.py` from `backend/app/sim/fixtures/validation.json`.

## Benchmark

[OpinionQA](https://github.com/tatsu-lab/opinions_qa) (Santurkar et al., 2023, *Whose Opinions Do Language Models Reflect?*): questions from 15 waves of the Pew Research Center's American Trends Panel, with respondent-level answers and survey weights. We use the seven waves on non-political topics:

| Wave | Topic |
|---|---|
| W27 | Automation and driverless vehicles |
| W34 | Biomedical and food issues |
| W41 | America in 2050 |
| W42 | Trust in science |
| W49 | Privacy and surveillance |
| W50 | Relationships and family |
| W54 | Economic inequality |

The raw data is **not** in this repo: the OpinionQA repository has no license and the American Trends Panel has its own terms. `scripts/fetch_opinionqa.py` downloads it to `data/opinionqa/` (gitignored). Only derived scores are committed. Data: Pew Research Center, American Trends Panel, as processed for OpinionQA. Pew Research Center bears no responsibility for the analyses or interpretations of the data presented here.

## Method

**Questions.** A question is eligible if its options are ordinal (OpinionQA's `option_ordinal` has more than one distinct position), it has 2–12 options, and it was put to the whole sample or to whole random forms. Skip-logic follow-ups are dropped, because their real answers come from a non-random subset that a full simulated sample can't match: for each form, a question must reach either at least 95% or at most 5% of respondents. A seeded, wave-stratified split sets aside a dev set (used only to check prompts and plumbing) and a test set (the reported numbers). Nothing was tuned on the test set.

**Groups.** Groups are defined on what an agent's card carries, and the real group distributions are recomputed from respondent-level data with each wave's weight (`WEIGHT_W<wave>`), so both sides use identical definitions:

| Attribute | Levels | Pew variable |
|---|---|---|
| Region | Northeast, Midwest, South, West | CREGION |
| Age | 18–29, 30–49, 50–64, 65+ (from the agent's exact age) | AGE |
| Gender | Female, male | SEX |
| Race / ethnicity | White, Black, Hispanic (any race), other (incl. Asian) | F_RACETHN |
| Education | HS or less, some college (incl. associate's), bachelor's, postgrad | EDUCATION (6 levels merged) |
| Income | Under $30k, $30k–100k, $100k+ | INCOME (middle bands merged) |
| Party ID | Democrat, Republican, independent (incl. "something else") | POLPARTY |
| Ideology | Liberal, moderate, conservative | POLIDEOLOGY (5 levels merged) |

OpinionQA's citizenship, marital status, religion and religious-attendance groups aren't on the agent card and aren't evaluated. Race uses Pew's race-ethnicity variable rather than OpinionQA's `RACE` column, because the card treats Hispanic as an ethnicity of any race.

**Population.** One synthetic population of U.S. adults, sampled with the app's stratified sampler using the benchmark's weighted margins as targets (Black, Hispanic, Asian and other respondents oversampled), then raked with the app's raking code to the benchmark's margins on all eight attributes. Every condition asks the same sampled people.

**Asking.** Questions go out in blocks of 10 per call through a `survey` run (the same lease-and-step protocol as every other simulation), with one fixed output schema (`SurveyBlock`, one option letter per question) so the cached persona is reused across blocks. The prompt is the question wording and lettered options, preceded by one line saying the survey was fielded in 2017–2019. Agents never see any real answer distribution.

**Conditions.**
1. **Interview agents**: the app's default agent, a model-written life-history interview plus four expert perspectives (Park et al., 2024), shown with the light card (name, age, gender, place, work).
2. **Card-only agents**: the full card (all demographics plus the seeded traits: occupation, Big Five, news diet, issues), no interview.
3. **Demographics-only prompt**: just the eight evaluated attributes, like OpinionQA's group-steering prompts.
4. **Uniform random**: every option equally likely.
5. **Real U.S. topline for every group**: the true overall distribution used as the prediction for every group. A simulator that captures how groups differ should beat this on group scores; one that only knows the topline can't.

Plus two references that aren't predictors: the **sampling ceiling** (real respondents drawn with probability proportional to weight, at the simulation's group sizes, scored against the full real group: the best a perfect simulator could do with this many agents) and **real groups vs the overall population** (OpinionQA's human baseline).

**Metric.** OpinionQA's representativeness (paper §3.2 and App. A.4; `helpers.get_max_wd` in their repo): for each question, the 1-D Wasserstein distance between the simulated and real answer distributions over the ordinal option positions, divided by the distance between the two extreme positions (N − 1 for a 1…N scale). Refusals are dropped and the rest renormalized. R = 1 − the mean distance over questions. Intervals are 95% percentile bootstraps over questions.

### Reading the results

**The simulated populations do not beat a uniform-random guess on these questions.** Card-only agents scored 0.824 overall against 0.820 for uniform random, with overlapping intervals; the demographics-only prompt scored 0.815 and interview agents, the app's default, scored lowest at 0.806. On group scores the picture is the same (mean group R 0.817 card-only, 0.807 demographics-only, 0.802 interview, 0.816 uniform). For reference, OpinionQA reported 0.70–0.82 for 2023 language models against the overall population on its full question set, so these numbers sit at the top of that range, but that range is not the bar that matters: on this question set a uniform guess already reaches 0.82.

**The main failure is too little disagreement.** Real respondents spread their answers (mean normalized entropy 0.84; the most common answer gets 55%). Simulated respondents pile onto one answer (entropy 0.43–0.52; the most common answer gets 74–80%), and they rarely pick the strongest options ("definitely", "a great deal", "very confident"). Interview agents are the most concentrated, which is why they score lowest: a model-written life story makes each agent more confident without making the population more varied. Some questions are also simply wrong in direction; on the dev set, agents picked "better chance" for couples who live together before marriage 0% of the time against 49% of real respondents.

**Simulated groups don't differ the way real groups do.** Predicting every group with the real overall answer distribution scores 0.966; no simulated condition comes close, and the sampling ceiling (0.969 mean group R with our group sizes) shows the gap isn't due to having too few agents per group. Republicans and conservatives are the worst-modeled groups for the two prompt-based conditions (0.775–0.800).

What this means for the app: treat simulated levels and splits as rough, and expect them to be too uniform within groups. The message tests compare arms on the same simulated people, which this benchmark doesn't measure; the classic experiments below are the closer check on that use, and there the direction of every effect with a published reference came out right, while sizes ranged from 0.02× (anchoring, where agents simply know the answer) to 2.3× (sunk cost).

These results are on Claude Haiku 5.5 at low effort through the Claude Code CLI, on synthetic personas; agents built from real people's interviews or survey answers (CSV import) were not tested here. Nothing was tuned on the test questions; the dev set was used once, to check the plumbing, and showed the same pattern.

## Reproducing

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/fetch_opinionqa.py
.venv/bin/python scripts/validate.py --dry-run
make shim   # or set ANTHROPIC_API_KEY and drop LLM_BACKEND
LLM_BACKEND=claude-cli SIM_CONCURRENCY=12 .venv/bin/python scripts/validate.py --split test
```

Runs resume from `data/validation/db.json` if interrupted. `--limit 5` is a smoke test.
